**Steal a Superpower** is a Roblox game by **11 R$ Productions**. You collect ability cards, battle through zones, defeat Dominus bosses, and put cards on base holders to generate Cash.

**The best way to begin is to work on combat and income together:** learn one card's moves while keeping eligible holders occupied. This guide combines the basic gameplay explanation with a practical first-session checklist.

The mechanics below are based on the [official Roblox game description](https://www.roblox.com/games/74183246285569/Steal-a-Superpower); specific card examples come from player-provided in-game screenshots. Exact costs, drop rates, damage thresholds, and time-to-clear have not been independently tested. “First session” is a suggested order, not a claim you can clear a zone within an hour.

## What to do first: five beginner decisions

| Your question | What to do | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What should I open first? | Inspect your available ability packs and cards. | Different cards have different moves. |
| What should I equip? | Compare each card's regular move, special move, and level. | Leveling unlocks stronger abilities. |
| How can I earn Cash while fighting? | Put an eligible card on an available base holder. | Holders can generate Cash during combat. |
| How do I reach the next zone? | Work toward defeating the current area boss. | Boss victories unlock areas and reward loot chests. |
| Which upgrade should I buy? | Identify whether damage, survivability, or movement is holding you back. | Power, Health, and speed are upgrade systems. |

## 1. Open packs and learn one usable card

Ability packs give you superpower cards. The game's description mentions powers such as **lightning, fire, frost, gravity, time, shadow, and gold**. You do **not** need the rarest card to start learning combat. First inspect the moves and levels of what you already own.

These example cards were documented in player-supplied gameplay screenshots:

- [Puncher](/powers/puncher/): Common; Power Jab and Mega Punch; displayed **$5/s**.
- [Brawler](/powers/brawler/): Common; Headbutt and Body Slam; displayed **$6/s**.
- [Hothead](/powers/hothead/): Uncommon; Fire Punch and Inferno Uppercut; displayed **$15/s**.

Displayed Cash income is **not** a ranking of combat strength. A card's moves and level matter too. Compare the [verified card index](/powers/verified-card-index/) and read [how superpower cards work](/powers/how-superpower-cards-work/).

## 2. Level cards to unlock stronger moves

According to the developer's description, leveling your card unlocks its most powerful ability. Before changing cards or buying upgrades, check whether your useful special move is still locked.

There is no verified universal unlock level or leveling cost for every card in this guide. Check the actual card information in your version of the game.

## 3. Start base income while you explore

Cards placed on **holders** in your base produce Cash, including while you fight. Before leaving for an extended zone or boss attempt, check whether your available holders are occupied.

Do not assume every card fits every holder, that earnings continue offline, or that displayed $/s values are identical at every upgrade level. Those details have not been confirmed here.

See [How Base Cash Works](/base/how-base-cash-works/).

## 4. Defeat bosses, open chests, and advance zones

Fight enemies with your equipped card and work toward the next **Dominus boss**. The developer says defeating a boss unlocks the next area and provides loot chests and packs.

After winning, **collect the chest rewards and inspect your new cards** before rushing to the next zone. New cards may improve your combat options or your base income. The official description names **Dominus Empyreus** as the final boss, but does not supply verified boss health figures or a full zone order.

For more, see [Dominus Bosses and Zones](/bosses/dominus-bosses-and-zones/).

## 5. Upgrade what is actually holding you back

Do not buy the next available upgrade automatically. Use your last attempt to find the bottleneck:

| Problem | Check first |
| --- | --- |
| You deal too little damage | Card level, unlocked moves, and Power upgrades |
| You are defeated too quickly | Health upgrades and clearly described temporary effects |
| Moving between fights is slow | Speed upgrades and trails available in your game |
| You cannot afford improvements | Base holders and Cash production |

The game also describes **auras**, critical hits, and kill-streak bonuses. Upgrade effects and costs can change, so inspect current in-game descriptions before spending. Read [Upgrades, Auras and Trails](/upgrades/auras-trails-and-stats/).

## 6. Collect Index rewards

Completing the **Index** gives permanent collection rewards tied to card rarities. A new card may therefore be valuable even if you do not equip it immediately. The developer also mentions Top Cash and Top Power leaderboards.

Check the current in-game Index for exact rarity requirements and rewards.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Leaving holders empty:** you miss a chance to earn Cash while exploring.
- **Choosing a card only for its displayed $/s:** income does not tell you its combat effectiveness.
- **Assuming special moves are unlocked immediately:** review card level requirements.
- **Repeating the same failed boss attempt:** identify whether damage, Health, mobility, or Cash is the issue.
- **Treating reported codes as guaranteed active:** use the [Codes page](/codes/) to distinguish official announcements from unverified claims.
- **Guessing upgrade prices:** check the current in-game shop before spending.
